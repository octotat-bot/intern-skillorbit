<!-- chunk id=jd-da-001 | title=Data analyst: typical responsibilities | topic=job-description | roles=data_analyst | sections=experience,projects | source=self-authored -->
Entry-level data analysts typically gather data from databases and spreadsheets, clean and validate it, write SQL queries to answer business questions, build recurring reports and dashboards, and present findings to non-technical stakeholders. Postings often mention working with sales, marketing or operations teams and defining KPIs.

<!-- chunk id=jd-da-002 | title=Data analyst: required skills | topic=job-description | roles=data_analyst | sections=skills | source=self-authored -->
Commonly required: strong SQL (joins, aggregations, window functions), Excel (pivot tables, lookups), a BI tool such as Tableau or Power BI, Python or R with pandas for analysis, and foundational statistics (descriptive statistics, hypothesis testing, A/B testing). Frequently preferred: data cleaning experience, ETL basics, data modeling, BigQuery or another cloud warehouse, and clear data storytelling.

<!-- chunk id=jd-da-003 | title=Data analyst: why SQL and statistics matter | topic=skill-gap | roles=data_analyst | sections=skills | source=self-authored -->
SQL is the most consistently listed analyst skill because most company data lives in relational databases; interviews usually include a live SQL exercise. Statistics matters because analysts must judge whether a change in a metric is real or noise, which is the basis of A/B testing and experiment analysis. Excel remains expected for quick ad-hoc analysis and sharing with business users.

<!-- chunk id=jd-da-004 | title=Data analyst: portfolio project ideas | topic=projects | roles=data_analyst | sections=projects | source=self-authored -->
Strong analyst portfolio projects use real public data and end in a decision: a sales dashboard in Power BI with KPI definitions, an SQL analysis of an e-commerce dataset answering five business questions, an A/B test analysis with a significance test and recommendation, or a cohort retention analysis in Python. Publish the queries or notebook on GitHub and summarize the insight in one sentence.

<!-- chunk id=jd-da-005 | title=Data analyst: example strong bullets | topic=bullets | roles=data_analyst | sections=projects,experience | source=self-authored -->
Examples of analyst bullets: "Wrote SQL queries joining 5 tables to identify the 3 product categories driving 60% of returns." "Built a Power BI dashboard tracking 12 KPIs, replacing a manual weekly Excel report." "Analyzed an A/B test of 20,000 users and recommended the variant with a 4% higher conversion rate."
